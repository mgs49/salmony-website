Top 10 Favorite Books

… see what else I like and dislike … 

(In no particular order)

  • O. Wilde (“The Picture of Dorian Gray”)
  • D. Adams (HHGG etc.)
  • M. Spark (“The Prime of Miss Jean Brodie”)
  • S. Nadolny (“Die Entdeckung der Langsamkeit”)
  • J. Salinger (“The Catcher in the Rye”)
  • M. Twain (“The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn”)
  • J. Steinbeck (e.g. “The Grapes of Wrath”)
  • I. Denisen (“Out of Africa” – wonderfully poetic, every paragraph to be read twice)
  • S. Zweig (esp. “Die Schachnovelle”)
  • S. Clarke (“Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell”)
  • S Montgomery (“The Soul of an Octopus”) – so interesting, poetic, moving, empathetic

Special mention in Children’s Category (also for adults):

  • Just so stories by Rudyard Kipling (but not “Kim”)
  • Alice in Wonderland by Lewis Carroll

The runners up

  •  E. Hemingway (“For Whom the Bell Tolls” – and only that one)
  • N. McGregor (“A History of the World in 100 Objects”) – best history of the world (and art, and humans) – podcast was best ever
  • M. Barbery “L’élégance du hérisson” a crypto-intellectual concierge and an upper class kid who wants to commit suicide  are bouleverséd by super elegant japanese man
  • S. Maugham (e.g. “Moon and Sixpence”, short stories like “The Three Fat Women of Antibes”, “Louise” etc.)
  • PG Wodehouse – all of his books (esp Much Obliged, Jeeves), the adorable audio versions on BBC and that he put the pages of the books he was writing up on his wall – the higher up, the better satisfied he was with the page. He would not release the books until all pages around the room were up to a certain “level”
  • G. Orwell (“1984” – better than Huxley’s “Brave New World” – should really be in Top10)
  • S. Thomas (“The End of Mr. Y” – the only book I have read twice over – in a row)
  • G. Greene (“Travels with my Aunt” – fabulous characters, insights into English life during my childhood (what a different world! but with surprisingly modern problems). But not the rabid religious/catholic books like the famous “The Power and the Glory”)
  • Philip K. Dick (“The Man in the High Castle” – fantastic utopia of the world if Japan and Germany had won the war, also wrote basis for Matrix, Blad Runner, Minority Report etc.)
  • F. Kafka (mini: Vor Dem Gesetz/Kaiserliche Botschaft, short: Die Verwandlung … all masterful)
  • C. Dickens (any – they are all much the same – except the dark, serious, no fun, no great characters, observations “Tale of Two Cities”)
  • Th. Hardy (ditto) – but also because of poems like “Hap”
  • M. Atwood (“The Handmaid’s Tale” – and no others)
  • J. Schalansky, “Der Hals der Giraffe” – beautiful prose, amazing biology facts, lovely character of protagonist
  • H. Melville (“Moby Dick”) – nautical, poetical, wonderful
  • David Mitchell (“Cloud Atlas”)
  • Ian McEwan (“Sweet Tooth” – master story teller at top of his craft with fantastic control of several plot turns & “The Children Act” beautifully crafted, wonderful play with words)
  • A. Camus (“The Plague”) … actually upon re-reading it during Covid-19 I am not so sure any more …
  • J. L. Carr (“A Month in the Country”) – slim novel to be read relaxed, reading each sentence twice to savour the prose
  • P. Süskind (“Das Parfüm”)
  • R. L. Stevenson (e.g.“Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde but not Kidnapped) – everything that Poe isn’t
  • D. Eagleman (“Sum”) – Fourty Tales of the Afterlife. Clever ideas by Neuroscientist what Bible could have evolved into
  • L. Shriver (“Should we stay or should we go”) – another clever book with alternative endings of life of a couple deciding to kill themselves at 80 … or not
  • A. Burgess (“A Clockwork Orange)
  • J.-W. Goethe (“Faust” – only part I)
  • I. Welsh (“Trainspotting”)
  • J. Boswell (“Life of Johnson”)
  • P. Theroux (the travel books such as “The Happy Isles of Oceania” – and only those)
  • M. Forster (“Have the Men had enough ?”)
  • O. Goldmsith (“The Vicar of Wakefield”)
  • A. Isler (“The Prince of West End Avenue”)
  • H. Lee (“To Kill a Mockingbird”)

The Non-Fiction Winners

  • Painting as a Pasttime, W. Churchill
  • Big Secrets, W. Poundstone
  • 21 Lessons for the 21st century, N. Harari – never made so many bookmarks in my life
  • All the Secrets of Magic revealed, H. Becker
  • Winzig, zäh und zahlreich – ein Bakterienatlas, Ludger Weß
  • Cheating at Bridge, J. Cameron
  • Das Verschwinden des Josef Mengele, O. Guez
  • The Curious Cook, McGee
  • Kitchen Confidential, Anthony Bourdain
  • Make your own Dinosaur out of Chicken Bones, C. McGowan
  • Instant Memory, R. Harbin
  • Hate Mail through the Ages, D. Carroll
  • Pavlov’s Heirs – Classical Psychology Experiments that Changed the Way we View ourselves, S. Schwartz
  • Home Workshop Explosives, Uncle Fester
    How to make Nitrogylcerin – in detail
  • The Deep – the extraordinary creatures of the Abyss, Claire Nouvian, and
    Planktonia, Erich Hoyt
    The unbelievable life in the deep oceans – great pictures and fascinating facts what we don’t know about our own world
  • Lexikon der populären Irrtümer, W. Krämer
  • all my publications (although some may count as fiction …)
